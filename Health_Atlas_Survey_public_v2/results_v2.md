# Results: waves 1 + 2 (NIKDU inpatients and outpatients)

99 completed forms: 59 inpatients (Sep 2026) and 40 outpatients (Oct 2026) at the National Institute of Kidney Diseases & Urology, Dhaka. Costs are for one illness from first care to the interview, including care before NIKDU. All figures are aggregates. Any figure based on fewer than 5 patients is suppressed. Money values are rounded to the nearest 100 BDT.

## 1. Key findings

- **Outpatients pay much less than inpatients, but the cost is still heavy.** Median medical cost is ৳26,700 for outpatients and ৳88,400 for inpatients (Mann-Whitney p < 0.001). That is about 1.1 months of household spending for outpatients and 4.1 months for inpatients.
- **About half of outpatient households face catastrophic spending.** 50% spent more than 10% of a year's household budget within 12 months (inpatients: 93%). At the 25% threshold the figures are 23% and 54%.
- **Outpatients borrow far less.** 38% of outpatients borrowed or sold assets, against 78% of inpatients (Fisher p < 0.001). Outpatients mostly paid from savings or income.
- **Cost-related delay is just as common.** 38% of outpatients and 44% of inpatients delayed coming to hospital because of cost. The difference is not significant.
- **Diagnostics are the largest cost.** Tests and imaging are 67% of itemised outpatient medical spending and 61% of inpatient spending.
- **Fragmented care goes with higher cost.** Each extra provider seen before NIKDU is associated with about 24% higher medical cost (p = 0.013), after controls.

## 6. Care-seeking path and Hypothesis 3

| | All | Inpatients | Outpatients |
|---|---|---|---|
| First care at a government hospital | 42% | 53% | 26% |
| First care at a private clinic/hospital | 39% | 36% | 45% |
| Came to NIKDU directly | 13% | 5% | 26% |
| Mean providers seen before NIKDU | 1.7 | 2.0 | 1.3 |
| Took medicine before any prescription | 28% | 43% | 5% |

**H3 as written** (pharmacy or informal provider first) **cannot be tested.** Only 4 of 99 patients started there, because a national referral hospital sees patients after they have passed through formal care.

**Regressions** (ln medical cost, robust SEs, n ≈ 94; controls: outpatient, dialysis, under 18, urology, ln months of care, ln nights, spending bracket):

| Model | Coefficient | p |
|---|---|---|
| Each extra provider before NIKDU | +0.22 (≈ +24%) | 0.013 |
| Private clinic first (vs government/direct) | +0.46 (≈ +59%) | 0.028 |
| Self-medicated before a prescription | +0.29 | 0.21 |
| Outpatient (vs inpatient) | −0.99 (≈ −63%) | 0.007 |

The provider and private-first results hold when the two highest-cost cases are dropped (p = 0.007 and 0.039). They are associations in a convenience sample: more complex illnesses may lead both to more providers and to higher cost.

## 7. Robustness

Two outpatient cases have very high costs. Without them, the overall median moves from ৳65,500 to ৳62,500 and the share over 10% from 75.3% to 74.7%. The outpatient median moves from ৳26,700 to ৳21,700 and the share over 10% from 50.0% to 47.4%. The outpatient **mean** falls by about half, so means are not reported.

## 8. Limitations

- Convenience samples at one public tertiary hospital; not nationally representative.
- Costs are recalled by respondents. Some forms gave totals only, or rates without durations; assumptions are logged in the private data.
- Household spending was collected in brackets, so catastrophic rates are approximate.
- No interview dates were recorded. Response rate is known for only part of wave 2: 20 completed out of 38 approached (53%).
- Dialysis costs appear under-reported for some outpatients, so dialysis figures are likely underestimates.
